# 21 — Interleaved drills

## Outcome

Retrieve and select skills without a module title revealing the intended tool,
then explain why alternatives were rejected.

## The 80/20 model

Blocked practice repeats one topic while its cues remain visible. It feels
smooth but can produce recognition rather than retrieval. Interleaving mixes
coercion, collections, async, errors, and data so you must diagnose the problem
class before choosing an approach.

Difficulty and productive forgetting are useful when followed by feedback.
Space repetitions across days. Record wrong predictions and solution-choice
errors, not just syntax mistakes. Rebuilding a partly forgotten mechanism is
often stronger learning than rereading its finished answer.

Do not interleave material never understood initially. First build a usable
mental model, then remove topic cues.

## Common traps

- Reading the answer immediately after mild friction.
- Measuring speed before correctness/selection.
- Random drills unrelated to project weaknesses.
- Repeating solved code from visual memory.
- Mixing so many concepts that failure becomes undiagnosable.

## Optimized exercises

1. **Cold set:** solve five shuffled tasks—boundary parse, grouping, closure,
   async ordering, and error translation—without their topic labels.
2. **Selection:** for five short scenarios, name two plausible approaches,
   choose one, and state the discriminating constraint before coding.
3. **Application:** one week after a RelayDesk feature, rebuild its critical
   pure mechanism from contract only, compare, and document forgotten details.

## Exit gate

On a mixed task, identify problem class, relevant constraints, likely failure,
and chosen tool before writing implementation code.
